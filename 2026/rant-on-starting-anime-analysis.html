Rant on starting anime analysis

1. Information is Narrative.

When you look at something for instance, if you look at say, a quote. The words themselves don’t actually mean anything. So let’s say you have a quote here:

“Words build bridges into unexplored regions.”

You know this quote in itself doesn’t actually mean anything, but it’s in the process of you essentially taking in the words and then reconceptualizing those words into a sentence and then you understanding the context–the context that has a quote–that you essentially created meaning to it. Now, you’ve created meaning to it, but what if I said for instance that this quote was said by this person:

“Words build bridges into unexplored regions.”

Adolf Hitler

Oh shit. Now you have this quote with this very particular meaning. So now the context of the quote probably changes. It now becomes this sort of dark ironic twist if you know much about what happens. But, for instance, if you actually consider that someone who has no idea of who this person is and they just read this quote then their experience is no less valid. However, their narrative, their understanding of the information is ultimately affected by what they know or have thus experienced with the information they have thus been presented. In other words, the process of deconstruction and then synthesis no longer has that context. And so we have to be very mindful that information is narrative. What you choose to talk about, what you choose to include, and what you choose to omit is very important, and you just have to be very mindful of that. And you also have to be very mindful of the people who are talking about these things as well. Because ultimately what I say about these sorts of stories are my narratives and what I sort of value and so we always have to be focused on that.

2. Analysis is a positive-sum.

The second one that I wanted to say is that all information adds to the general discourse pool. Now, when you provide something, don’t worry about it being innovative. Don’t worry about it being radically innovative. Just provide what you think works. Even if it’s been covered by somebody else. So let’s provide an example. For instance, if you take a look at like say someone makes a Naruto video and then another person makes another Naruto video and then these two videos are exactly the same, well the question now becomes, well why is the second Naruto video that necessarily worthwhile if it’s a piece that is exactly the same? Let’s put aside the whole probable plagiarism thing if they are so very close, if they are exactly the same. The second video in itself can still be a worthwhile piece primarily because after a passage of time we can still think, well why would this video still be relevant? That it would be worth producing? Or are these arguments still focused and relevant enough that even after all these episodes have come out, after more episodes, more information everything has come out that we can still contextualize the argument to be very valid for what’s being said here. Even after the Zeitgeist or the paradigm has changed or the research methods have changed to come to that conclusion still is still very interesting. And even if the person still has no idea of any of the changes that have occurred even if they have no idea that any of these positions emerged. Why? And that itself is a worthwhile understanding or a worthwhile point of trying to understand something in an analysis of something that really has already happened.

So I would say that don’t worry so much about trying to provide this radically innovative argument. Don’t worry too much about trying to find something meaningful. Just put something out there. And ultimately it will add to the larger pool of critical discourse. Whether people agree to it or not is something I can’t guarantee is going to happen. But I can guarantee that it will in some metric be worthwhile and it’s up to whomever consumes it to really decide how worthwhile it is. So just put it out there.

3. Do a Literature Review

Third one that I want to mention here is do a literature review. Literature reviews are incredibly important because they contextualize and frame different narratives of understanding works that you may not agree with or you may actively disagree with. But they are still nevertheless important because they may touch upon particular subjects or ideas in particular ways that can still be worthwhile. And well, if you take a look at literature reviews, they themselves could be really helpful in framing narratives that you may miss.

My favorite movie is the 1977 absurdist Japanese horror film House by Nobuhiko Obayashi. Now when I first saw House I really did not care much about it. I really thought it was one of those sort of nondescript but funny strange comedies. But, as I read more about it as I read video essays on House as I consumed more information about Obayashi and his background and what he intended for House, it changed my understanding of the film and the signifiers in the film essentially morphed into something that had a very different resonance with me. It went from essentially this really camp horror story to this viscerally emotionally bare and very vulnerable piece and it was only because I essentially went off and did a literature review on that movie itself that it essentially changed my understanding of it. And people might say well yeah you know that’s only because you went off and researched about it. If you had watched it and not done any research about it then it could have possibly failed as a message of the piece itself. That is a very valid concern. But by going off and doing that literature about it, I have a more holistic understanding of the piece itself and therefore I have more of an agency as a being who creates his own narrative of the information I’m presented. Essentially, in other words, I have gained more information and thus I can make a more confirming judgement as to what I value about it. And well, it was significant enough for me to change my understanding about it. But I mean at the end of the day these points really only matter for anime analysis or analysis in general these points really only matter if you decide to do something about it. And I think that really the most important point, aside from these, like the most important point to me is just do it.
