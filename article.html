  • Physical Principles for Scaling Neural Decoding
    • Let’s say you want to build magical brain interfaces.
    The main bottlenecks are:
      1. Information processing and transmission load
      2. Heat generated from (1)
      3. Volume displacement from building devices from (1) and (2)
    Mechanistic interpretations: The number of bits needed to record every single neuron in the human brain per second can be measured. Total data rate = number of neurons in a human brain * frequency of sampling * bits per sample = 86 billion neurons * 1000Hz * 1 bit per sample = 86 trillion bits per second = 11 Tb / second *that’s a lot of bits!! Limitations: information compression and transmission. Human brain tissue can withstand 2°C change in temperature over hours, before serious tissue damage. Instantaneous change in temperature of brain tissue wrt time = heat transfer of deep brain tissue / brain tissues heat capacity. Heat transfer of brain tissue: = (applied power + power per unit mass of basal metabolism) - density*heat capacity*volume of rate of flow*change in temperature of blood Solve for applied power to get the heat generated must be less than 2.5 times the energy generated by the human brain. *that’s a lot of heat!! Limitations: heat generated from electrodes, neural recording, processing, and transmission. The volume of electrodes and hardware implanted into the brain must displace <1% of the total brain. Constraints: Number of neurons a single electrode can distinguish from one another. Diameter of the wire. The wire can’t be too thin, otherwise it isn’t physically feasible to construct it without it giving unreliable signals. But the number of electrodes can’t be too little, otherwise we can’t distinguish the neurons from one another. *that’s a lot of wires in your brain!! Limitations: the hardware implants connected to the brain has to displace <1% of total brain volume, before sustaining permanent, irreversible brain damage. Great! Now you have a somewhat broad understanding of what brain computer interface researchers are consistently asking themselves every time they build new select modalities to understand our thoughts.

    Quantum systems are composed of qubits, wires, and quantum gates. Just as a classical computer can be configurable by different physical systems, quantum systems operate and behave differently using different components, but the underlying principles are the same. A transition through a quantum wire represents a passage in time. Qubits can be represented by any particle, e.g. a proton, photon, neutrino. However, some particles are more suited to be qubits than wires and vice versa. Neutrinos have weak interactions with other matter, so it is hard for them to manipulate stable systems. Thus, neutrinos are suitable to be used as quantum wires but poor candidates to be used as qubits.

    In classical computing, bits encode information 0 and 1s. In quantum computing, qubits store quantum states, defined as two dimensional unit vectors living in complex space. The vector is composed from a linear combination of elementary states, the zero ket and the one ket.

    An arbitrary qubit can be expressed as a linear combination of the computational basis, with alpha and beta being complex scalar values. Suppose Alice is a physicist who is experimenting with the applications of quantum computing. Alice is interested in encoding her message to Bob using binary expansion of a real number alpha. In theory, Bob can decode the binary value of alpha from measuring the output of the quantum circuit, and thus, a single qubit can encode an infinitely long string of binary numbers. One of the reasons why quantum computing is so difficult to implement, despite its promises to leverage qubits for greater compute power, is because a major constraint of the system is that the output of quantum circuits are measured through classical means. The value of alpha and beta is very hard to decode. There is no way to find alpha and beta if they start out unknown.

    We can represent quantum systems algebraically, with quantum circuits, and with matrices. Simple quantum logic gates include the NOT gate, which in quantum mechanics is termed the X gate after Wolfgang Pauli’s Sx notation became irrelevant, and the H gate. The hope of the H gate is to be able to reverse engineer the state of qubit that wouldn’t otherwise be possible on a computational basis.

    We learned about quantum circuits, NOT and H gate, the properties of a quantum wire, the algebraic representation, the circuit diagram representation, and the matrix representation of quantum circuits, superposition is just a fancy word for a linear combination, X gate in quantum computing is has the same role as a NOT gate in classical computing, a quantum state is a two dimensional unit vector, denoted by zero ket and one ket, and can be initialized with complex numbers alpha and beta, note that the zero ket is not the same as a zero vector, measuring alpha is useful for encoding and decoding binary values into qubits, it took quantum physicists the first quarter of the 20th century to come up with a precise mathematical description of a quantum state, it is really difficult to wrap our heads around how particles are interacting at the quantum level, no dominant interpretation exists, the probability of measuring a 0 or 1 from the output of a quantum circuit is measured by the square of the coefficients alpha and beta, the sum of the probabilities should equal one.

    We introduced the idea of unitary matrices, or matrices that preserve the length of a vector. Taking the complex transpose of an arbitrary quantum state ket produces a bra, which we denote as <psi|. The matrix product of a unitary matrix and its (Hermitian) conjugate, given by the dagger notation, rotates the vector in space, but preserves the length of the vector. This notation is called the Dirac notation, or the bra-ket notation. Mjk = <ej| M |ek>. In other words, finding the entry of a matrix is the same as applying the elementary row vector ej to the matrix M, followed by applying the column vector ek. The norm of ket is the square root of the product of the bra-ket. Using this definition, we can venture into gates involving two qubits.

    The computational basis states for 2 qubit system is |00>, |01>, |10>, and |11>. The CNOT gate is the quantum equivalent of the NOT gate for two gates. However, this flipping mechanism is used as follows: define the control qubit and target qubit. Suppose |x> is the control and |y> is the target. Then, |xy> -> |x,y (circle with a plus sign inside) x>. This means, we are modulo the addition of x, y. If x is 0, the switch is off, thus the state of y doesn’t change. If x is 1, then y flips. Next, we introduced the Toffoli gate. This is the equivalent of the AND gate in classical computing. Because, as we stated earlier, qubits have to be unitary and reversible, (x ^ y) is very difficult to implement in quantum computing. The NAND gate can be constructed by taking apart the z component of the Toffoli gate and replacing z with 1. To review, quantum computing consists of three parts: the input being the initial computational basis state, passing through a quantum circuit composed of CNOT gate and quantum gates, whose output is measured by a classical bit. The hope of quantum computing is to be able to simulate any physical system. But so far physicists are unable to bridge the gap between the standard model with Einstein’s general relativity. John Preskill and his collaborators have been able to make progress on using quantum computing to simulate quantum field theory. The CIA and nations have been interested in the development of quantum computing to crack encryptions since the mid 1990s in hopes of gathering intelligence on foreign nations and its own citizens. An already proven progress on the fruits of quantum computing is Shor’s quantum factorization algorithm. We are able to factorize any positive real number, in the hundreds of digits, to its prime factors in polynomial time versus the best classical algorithms that can only do so in sub-exponential time. The research and development of quantum algorithms hopes to simulate the movement, position, and complex behaviors of atoms and molecules that would otherwise take classical computers too long to simulate. Atom behavior at the quantum level is very poorly simulated by chemists and drug discovery is slow because classical computing is poor at simulating things at the quantum level.
